Milestone: **#18 — v0.21 — Governance as schema**. Release gate: **#478**.

Named product outcome: **One machine-readable governance contract can be
inspected, diffed, provisioned, validated, and used to compile approved plans
into GitHub state.**

Spark taught repository governance partly through prose and partly through
individual commands, so an agent could hold the doctrine correctly and still
recreate labels, priorities, milestone conventions and metadata rules
inconsistently from repo to repo. This release replaces that with one versioned
authority for **allowed shapes** — never for project-specific judgment.

## What shipped

**A versioned governance artifact** —
`preferences/governance-models/spark-default.tsv`. Label families with their
cardinality and requirement; execution structure with an explicit
`authoritative`/`derived` verdict per surface; the separations that must not be
collapsed; provisionable surfaces; mechanically assessable enforcement. It is
line-oriented and tab-separated rather than nested JSON, for the reason
[ADR-0030](../adr/0030-governance-model-representation.md) records: the
preference resolver is deliberately flat, and the zero-dependency rule means a
nested-JSON authority would be unreadable wherever `jq` is absent — "degrades
gracefully" cannot mean "degrades to no governance model at all". Verified with
a `jq`-free `PATH`.

**Resolution keeps the three tiers**, later winning, every record naming its
source. A tier declaring any member of a family replaces that family's whole
member set, because a per-member merge can only add and never remove. Removing a
member prunes the lower tiers' rules about it rather than bricking the model.

**`docs-impact`** — every issue declares what documentation its change affects,
and the declaration is verified against the issue's implementation evidence. The
failure mode closed is not "a PR changed no docs"; it is **silent** docs impact,
where an omission is indistinguishable from a deliberate "none". Evidence is the
union across every merged-or-open linked PR, aggregated then judged, from the
paginated files endpoint and including rename sources.

**The governance engine** — `inspect`, `diff`, `apply`, `validate` over one row
generator, so a rule cannot have two authorities and the four cannot disagree.
`spark labels` is an explicit compatibility wrapper over the same rows.

**The plan compiler** — `issue-manifest.sh` extended, not replaced, with
`milestone`, `update`, `order` and `decision` records, plus cycle detection and
a live diff; `spark plan validate|diff|apply|verify` is its command surface.
Structure stays the script's, meaning the schema's.

**Integration** — `doctor` reports governance readiness and names what it
defers; selection refuses mechanically invalid execution metadata; `brief` and
`resume` surface readiness from deterministic state.

## The self-hosting rule, and how it was made satisfiable

This milestone introduces `docs-impact` and is the first held to it. **#470 is
the sole bootstrap exception** — it had to close before the family could exist.

The gate initially could not satisfy its own rule. Its sub-issue order placed
#483 last, and `spark next` reads that order as the tie-breaker among equally
prioritized eligible issues — so #471, #472 and #473 would all have closed
before `docs-impact` existed, while the rule named exactly those three. The
order was corrected to `470, 483, 471, 472, 473` on 2026-08-27 with the native
dependency graph and every priority left untouched, and the reconciliation is
recorded on #478.

The result is verifiable rather than asserted: #483 closed before #471, #472 and
#473, and each of those four carries `docs-impact:reference` satisfied against
its own branch. Nothing was retrofitted.

## What the withdrawn certification checked, and where it was hollow

Recorded as-was, because the gap between these claims and the findings above is
the useful part. Verified against merged `master` at `8c3c7c3` — but see the
annotations:

- **Scope** — every leaf closed; only the gate remains open. **No longer true:**
  #470, #483, #472 and #473 have since been reopened.
- **Schema** — a `version` record resolves; `governance.model` selects it as a
  scalar preference rather than a fourth configuration layer; all three tiers
  are read and reported.
- **Allowed shapes only** — the shipped artifact contains no issue numbers and
  no per-issue verdicts.
- **Read-only** — `inspect`, `diff` and `validate` leave the working tree
  untouched; `apply` previews and needs `--yes`; drift repair needs
  `--repair-drift` on top of that; a judgment row is never applied.
- **One authority per rule** — `spark labels` delegates to the shared row
  generator; `spark plan` delegates to the one compiler. **Falsified by #511:**
  a cross-tier `exclusive` override leaves one rule with two answers, because
  consumers disagree about which member is exclusive.
- **`docs-impact`** — family and path mapping are schema data; the labels are
  provisioned on the remote.
- **Self-hosting** — #471, #472, #473, #483 each declare and satisfy a
  disposition; #470 declares none, as the recorded exception.
- **v0.20 shipped first** — `v0.20.0` is an ancestor of `master`.
- `spark doctor` reports 0 errors. `tests/run.sh` passes all 48 suites **in an
  environment that has `gh`** — which is the caveat criterion 13 does not carry,
  and #516 is the consequence.

**Where it was hollow.** The criterion-10 check grepped for a function name and
a comment string, so it verified that the words were still present rather than
that the properties held — #515 and #518 both violate exactly those properties.
The read-only checks confirmed the verbs do not *write*; nothing confirmed that
a PASS *means* anything, which is #512, #517 and #520.

## Known state at release, reported rather than hidden

`spark governance validate` **fails on this repository**, and that is the
correct answer:

- **#508 carries two category labels** (`bug` and `infrastructure`) and has no
  release disposition.
- **Milestone-assigned issues predating `docs-impact`** carry no disposition.

Both are real governance findings about the backlog, not defects in the check.
Spark reports them and refuses to guess; deciding each one is the human's.

Seven category labels also **drift** from the model — they were created before
the schema existed, so their colours and descriptions never matched. Reported as
`~` and deliberately not repaired: an existing label is the project's own
decision, and repair needs `--repair-drift` said out loud.

## Defects found during the release's own validation

Recorded because the count is the useful part. Across five implementation
cycles, adversarial review and self-attack found **31 reasoning defects** in new
code, while Spark's deterministic gates caught **12 mechanical omissions** —
missing reference sections, line and byte budgets, a tier-boundary violation, a
commit subject over length, and a shipped/shipped taxonomy drift. The split was
one-directional: not one gate caught a reasoning defect, and not one review
finding was a mechanical omission.

Two classes are worth naming because they recurred *after* being fixed and
documented:

- **A green result that means "not looked at."** Found in `apply`, then shipped
  again in `verify`, then again in doctor's file-surface count — three separate
  instances across three cycles, each after the previous had been fixed and
  written up.
- **An assertion that cannot fail.** Five instances, including one where the
  test written to prove a behaviour was what concealed its regression.

Certification itself found one more: the trunk-policy surface was emitted as
"not assessed", and `apply`'s refusal on any unread surface then disabled the
verb entirely. A deliberate deferral to a named verb is not an unknown; it has
its own status now, and `apply`'s guard is scoped to the surface it acts on.

## Release discipline

No v0.21 branch merged to `master` before `v0.20.0` was published. Every change
was issue-backed, branched, reviewed and merged through its own pull request
with a normal merge commit; nothing was squashed and no history was rewritten.

Release Please PR **#506** proposes `v0.21.0` and **must not be merged** in this
state. Its notes reconcile against the range — one `0.21.0` section from the
`v0.20.0` boundary, and 33 visible release-note commits against 33 unique
generated entries — but note that entry counting alone would not have detected
an *omission*; the commit-count reconciliation is what establishes that.
